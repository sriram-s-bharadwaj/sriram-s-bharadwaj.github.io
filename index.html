Sriram Bharadwaj

PhD Candidate in Theoretical Physics, University of California, Los Angeles

I am a final-year PhD candidate in Theoretical Physics at UCLA. I am fortunate to be advised by Eric D’Hoker and Leo Zhou, and to be working with Di Luo and Zhongbo Kang. Previously, I received an MSc in Mathematical and Theoretical Physics from the University of Oxford and a BS in Mathematics/Applied Science from UCLA.

My research focuses on quantum algorithms and the quantum simulation of strongly interacting many-body systems. During my PhD, I have also worked on formal aspects of quantum field theory, which inform my approach to constructing physically faithful and resource-efficient Hamiltonian formulations.

  • Quantum simulation of many-body systems and lattice gauge theories: I develop Hamiltonian formulations suited to quantum simulation and study their phase structure and dynamics using classical methods, including tensor networks and neural quantum states, as well as variational quantum algorithms.
  • Quantum algorithms and optimization: I study quantum algorithms for continuous optimization and ground-state preparation. In work on CV-QAOA, we establish rigorous convergence guarantees and black-box quantum-classical separations for continuous optimization problems. I also analyze adiabatic algorithms for preparing ground states of bosonic and lattice gauge theory Hamiltonians, with provable error and runtime guarantees.

Recent projects include confinement vs. screening in the Schwinger model in curved space using MPS/DMRG, quantum simulations of topological phases in (2+1)D lattice QED, and variational and adiabatic quantum algorithms for lattice gauge theories.
