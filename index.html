Sriram Bharadwaj

PhD Candidate in Theoretical Physics, University of California, Los Angeles

Bio. I am a fifth-year PhD student in Theoretical Physics at UCLA. I am fortunate to be advised by Eric D’Hoker and Leo Zhou, and to be mentored by Di Luo and Zhongbo Kang. Previously, I received an MSc in Mathematical and Theoretical Physics from the University of Oxford and a BS in Mathematics/Applied Science from UCLA.

My research lies at the intersection of strongly coupled quantum field theory, lattice gauge theory, and quantum information. During my Ph.D., I have studied both formal aspects of quantum field theory and computational approaches to strongly coupled gauge theories, with a particular emphasis on tensor-network methods and variational quantum algorithms.

A central theme of my work is the Hamiltonian formulation of lattice gauge theories. I am interested in developing physically faithful lattice Hamiltonian formulations of gauge theories that are suitable for classical and quantum simulation. This perspective is useful in regimes where conventional Euclidean Monte Carlo methods face fundamental obstacles, such as finite density, real-time dynamics, or topological terms.

More broadly, my work explores how Hamiltonian formulations and quantum algorithms can be combined to study strongly coupled quantum field theories in regimes that are difficult to access with existing classical methods. Recent projects include: confinement vs. screening in the Schwinger model in curved space using MPS/DMRG, quantum simulations of topological phases in (2+1)D lattice QED, and variational/adiabatic quantum algorithms for lattice gauge theories.
