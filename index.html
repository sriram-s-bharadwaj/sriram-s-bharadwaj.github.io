Sriram Bharadwaj

PhD Candidate in Theoretical Physics, University of California, Los Angeles

I am a fifth-year PhD student in Theoretical Physics at UCLA. I am fortunate to be advised by Eric D’Hoker and Leo Zhou, and to be working with Di Luo and Zhongbo Kang. Previously, I received an MSc in Mathematical and Theoretical Physics from the University of Oxford and a BS in Mathematics/Applied Science from UCLA.

My research lies at the intersection of strongly coupled quantum field theory, lattice gauge theory, and quantum information. During my Ph.D., I have studied both formal aspects of quantum field theory (including conformal field theories, dualities, and topological defects) and computational approaches to strongly coupled gauge theories using tensor networks and quantum algorithms. A central theme of my work is developing physically faithful Hamiltonian formulations of lattice gauge theories that are suitable for classical and quantum simulation, especially in regimes where conventional Euclidean Monte Carlo methods faces a sign problem, such as at finite density or with topological terms. My current work focuses on the interplay between efficient Hamiltonian formulations and quantum algorithms.

Recent projects include confinement vs. screening in the Schwinger model in curved space using MPS/DMRG, quantum simulations of topological phases in (2+1)D lattice QED, and variational/adiabatic quantum algorithms for lattice gauge theories.
