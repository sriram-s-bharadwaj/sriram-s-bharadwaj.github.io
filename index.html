About

I am a final-year PhD candidate in Theoretical Physics at UCLA. I am co-advised by Eric D’Hoker and Leo Zhou, and work closely with Di Luo and Zhongbo Kang. Previously, I received an MSc in Mathematical and Theoretical Physics from the University of Oxford and a BS in Mathematics/Applied Science from UCLA.

Research

My research focuses on quantum algorithms and the quantum simulation of strongly coupled many-body systems, with a particular emphasis on lattice gauge theories. My work on formal aspects of quantum field theory informs my approach to constructing physically faithful and resource-efficient Hamiltonian formulations.

Sriram Bharadwaj

PhD Candidate in Theoretical Physics
University of California, Los Angeles

  • Quantum simulation of many-body systems and lattice gauge theories. To study phase structure and dynamics in regimes where conventional Monte Carlo methods suffer from sign problems (e.g., at finite density or in the presence of topological terms), I develop Hamiltonian lattice formulations of quantum many-body systems. I use a combination of classical methods (e.g., ED, tensor networks, and neural quantum states) and variational/adiabatic quantum algorithms to access physics beyond the reach of conventional classical approaches.
  • Quantum algorithms and optimization. To address many-body simulation and optimization problems beyond the reach of classical methods, I design efficient quantum algorithms for continuous optimization and ground-state preparation. In work on CV-QAOA, we establish rigorous convergence guarantees and black-box quantum-classical separations. I also analyze adiabatic algorithms for preparing ground states of bosonic and lattice gauge theory Hamiltonians.
